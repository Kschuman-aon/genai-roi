### Prologue – A Number That Looked Too Good

Meridian Mutual had given 180 software engineers an internal GenAI assistant
for six months. Maya Chen, the company's director of developer productivity,
had collected enthusiastic surveys and calculated a projected annual benefit
of $2.4 million. Then a larger-than-expected technology invoice reached Elena
Brooks, Meridian's chief financial officer, and exposed a question the pilot
dashboard could not answer: *what value had the company actually bought?*

| Case detail | Starting point |
|---|---|
| Pilot scope | 180 software engineers |
| Initial claim | $2.4 million annual benefit |
| Evidence behind the claim | Self-reported time saved, multiplied by loaded labor cost |
| Missing from the claim | Usage attribution, full cost, quality effects, risk, and confidence ranges |
| Decision required | Stop, continue, or expand the assistant |

### Epilogue – What Made the Analysis Defensible?

The team did not rescue the original claim. They replaced it with a smaller,
testable claim whose definitions, evidence, and uncertainty were visible.
That shift changed ROI from a promotional ratio into a management system: one
that could guide optimization, allocation, governance, and future investment.

| Challenge | How the team responded | Lesson for today |
|---|---|---|
| Benefits came from self-reported time | Rebuilt a baseline and used matched-team operational evidence | Treat surveys as evidence inputs, not cash-equivalent outputs |
| AI spend could not be assigned to work | Tagged requests by team, task, model class, tokens, and outcome | Unit economics require usage and cost attribution |
| Optimization could have reduced quality | Tested caching, context limits, and routing against fixed guardrails | Savings count only when the required outcome still holds |
| The first model omitted indirect cost | Built a full year-one TCO boundary | Define the cost boundary before calculating ROI |
| A single forecast hid uncertainty | Risk-adjusted benefits and tested downside assumptions | Show which variables can reverse the decision |
| Executives needed a choice, not a data dump | Led with options, recommendation, conditions, and stop rules | An ROI report succeeds when it supports action |

### Call to Action

Choose one GenAI initiative in your organization and write its measurement
contract before recalculating anything. If you cannot trace cost to a workflow,
name the counterfactual, and show the quality and risk conditions, the next
step is not a better slide—it is better evidence.
