## Why This Intelligent Textbook

Generative AI is now embedded in software development, data science, and customer-facing workflows across nearly every industry — and enterprises are pouring unprecedented amounts of money into it. Yet a striking number of the people writing the checks cannot answer a basic question their own finance leadership will ask: *is this actually paying off?*

**Across the enterprise landscape (2025–2026):**

- MIT's Project NANDA found that **95% of generative AI pilots** deliver no measurable impact on the bottom line within roughly six months of deployment, despite an estimated $30–40 billion in enterprise investment[^1]
- **56% of CEOs** report zero measurable revenue or cost benefit from their organization's AI investment, according to PwC's 29th Annual Global CEO Survey of 4,454 chief executives across 95 countries[^2]

**Worldwide:**

- Gartner forecasts worldwide AI spending will reach **$2.59 trillion in 2026** — a 47% increase year-over-year[^3]
- The share of companies allocating more than 5% of their total technology budget to AI has nearly doubled in 18 months to **34%**, and reaches **52%** among companies with more than $10 billion in annual revenue, per McKinsey's State of AI research[^4]

These numbers describe a field spending at an extraordinary rate while, by its own admission, mostly failing to measure whether that spending works. If you're an engineer, data scientist, or technical manager whose organization is part of that spending — this gap is now part of your job, whether or not anyone gave it a name yet.

This book takes a fundamentally different approach than a slide deck of best practices. It is built on a **learning graph of 561 interconnected concepts** organized into 13 categories, moving from what a token literally is, through the financial vocabulary (ROI, TCO, NPV, payback period) a technologist needs to speak fluently, into concrete cost-optimization techniques across the full software and data science lifecycle, and finally into the mechanics of writing an executive-ready ROI report. Concepts are introduced strictly in prerequisite order — you are never asked to understand something before its foundations have been built. The entire book is **open and free to read** — no paywalls, no access codes.

## How to Use This Book

This textbook is designed for self-paced study by practicing IT professionals. Each chapter builds directly on earlier ones, so reading in order is recommended, especially early on. The book is organized around:

- **27 chapters** spanning GenAI foundations and token economics through cost optimization, vendor economics, risk/governance, and executive financial reporting
- A **Glossary** of 561 ISO 11179-style definitions covering every concept in the learning graph
- **Quizzes** and **Annotated References** (Wikipedia, credited textbooks, and verified online resources) attached to each chapter as its content is finished
- A **Learning Graph** you can explore interactively to see how any concept connects to the rest of the book
- **Ledger**, a mascot who flags key ideas, warns about common pitfalls, and celebrates milestones — never decoration, always a signal worth pausing on

!!! note "This book is under active development"
    Chapter *structure* exists for all 27 chapters, but full content, quizzes, and references are being written and published chapter by chapter. Completed chapters currently include Chapter 1 (Core Concepts of Large Language Models) and Chapter 2 (Prompting, Deployment, and Model Optimization Basics). Check back as more chapters are finished.

The [Learning Graph](learning-graph/index.md) visualizes how all 561 concepts connect across chapters. If you want to check prerequisites for a specific topic before diving in, start there.
